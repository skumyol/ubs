## 3.3 Text Classification Categories

Each document or paragraph should be classified into one or more categories:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Oil Supply Disruption | War, sanctions, attacks, shipping routes, production cuts |
| Oilfield Cost Pressure | Freight, raw materials, project delay, labor, logistics |
| Grid Resilience | Transmission, substations, transformers, grid upgrade |
| Electricity Demand | Data centers, EVs, AI, industrial electrification, cooling |
| Policy-Backed Capex | Government infrastructure, grid investment, national security |
| Margin / Earnings Risk | Cost inflation, utilization risk, pricing pressure |

## 3.4 AI Workflow

### Step 1 — Data Collection

Create a spreadsheet with the following fields:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Source Name | Company / report / news source |
| Date | Publication date |
| Document Type | Transcript, report, article, filing |
| Company / Sector | Sieyuan, Halliburton, Grid, Oilfield Services |
| Text Excerpt | Paragraph or section |
| URL / Source | Source reference |
| Manual Notes | Human comments |

---

### Step 2 — Text Cleaning

Clean the text:

- Remove boilerplate
- Remove legal disclaimers
- Split into paragraphs
- Remove duplicate articles
- Standardize company names
- Keep only investment-relevant sections

---

### Step 3 — AI Classification

Prompt the AI to classify each paragraph.

Example prompt:

```text
Classify the following paragraph into one or more categories:

1. Oil Supply Disruption
2. Oilfield Cost Pressure
3. Grid Resilience
4. Electricity Demand
5. Policy-Backed Capex
6. Margin / Earnings Risk

Also assign:
- sentiment: positive, neutral, negative
- confidence score: 1 to 5
- reason in one short sentence

Paragraph:
[insert paragraph]
```

---

### Step 4 — Human Review

Human review is mandatory.

Check for:

* Misclassification
* Overstated sentiment
* Source reliability
* Duplicate news
* AI hallucination
* Lack of numerical support

---

Create:

1. Keyword heatmap
2. Category frequency chart
3. Timeline of signal shifts
4. Long vs short exposure comparison
5. Transcript keyword table

Example output:

| -------------------- | -------------: | ----------------: |
| Grid upgrade         |           High |               Low |

| Policy-backed capex  |           High |            Medium |
| Logistics disruption |            Low |              High |
| Margin pressure      |         Medium |              High |
| Project delay        |            Low |              High |

---

### Step 6 — Investment Interpretation

Convert AI findings into investment conclusion:

> Our AI-assisted text analysis suggests that public energy-security discussion remains dominated by oil disruption, while capex-related language is increasingly tied to grid resilience, electrification, and electricity continuity. This supports our long Sieyuan / short oilfield-service pair.

## 3.5 AI Advantages

* Processes more documents than a student team can manually read
* Identifies repeated language patterns across transcripts, reports, and news

* Makes qualitative signals more measurable
* Speeds up first-pass research

## 3.6 AI Limitations

Also mention clearly:

* AI cannot verify whether management guidance is accurate
* AI may over-weight frequently repeated media narratives
* AI classification depends on prompt design

* Sentiment does not equal earnings impact

# 7. Q&A Defense Plan

Prepare answers for the questions judges are likely to ask.

---

## Q1. Why not long oil if energy logistics are vulnerable?

**Answer:**

Because higher oil prices do not automatically create durable earnings upside for oilfield-service companies. Disruption can raise logistics costs, delay projects, and reduce operating visibility. Our view is that the more durable response to energy insecurity is investment in electricity continuity and grid resilience.

---

## Q2. Why Sieyuan rather than Dongfang Electric or Yantai Jereh?

**Answer:**

Sieyuan offers cleaner exposure to grid equipment and electricity infrastructure. Dongfang Electric has broader power-generation exposure, while Yantai Jereh remains more connected to oil and gas services. Since our thesis is about the shift from fuel security to electricity security, Sieyuan is the cleanest long.

---

## Q3. Why short Halliburton / oilfield services instead of an oil producer?

**Answer:**

Oil producers may benefit directly from oil price spikes. Oilfield-service companies are more exposed to customer capex timing, utilization, logistics, and project execution. That makes them a better short candidate for the view that the market is over-simplifying the benefit of oil disruption.

---

## Q4. What would make your thesis wrong?

**Answer:**

The thesis would be wrong if oilfield-service pricing power rises faster than logistics costs, if upstream capex accelerates significantly, or if grid capex is delayed. We monitor this through earnings revisions, order backlog, margin trends, and capex guidance.

---

## Q5. Is this a macro pitch or a stock pitch?

**Answer:**

It starts with a macro dislocation, but the trade is stock-specific. Sieyuan has direct exposure to grid equipment and overseas growth, while the short leg has specific margin and operating risks. The pair trade isolates the earnings-quality gap within the broader energy-security theme.
