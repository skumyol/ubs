Data centers are driving more power equipment orders than ever before.
Data centers generated more orders for GE Vernova’s electrification equipment in the first quarter of 2026 than in all of 2025 combined, the company disclosed this week in its first-quarter earnings call.
GE Vernova’s electrification segment produces substations, switchgear, transformers and high-voltage direct current systems. Total electrification orders essentially doubled year over year to $7.1 billion, with equipment orders in North America and Asia roughly tripling. Orders from this segment tied to data center customers reached $2.4 billion in Q1.
On the gas power side, GE Vernova signed 21 gigawatts (GW) of new gas turbine agreements in the first quarter, spanning customers in the U.S., Vietnam, Mexico, Brazil and Canada. Total GW under contract grew from 83 to 100 sequentially. The company’s backlog rose from 40 to 44 GW, and slot reservation agreements climbed from 43 to 56 GW. GE Vernova shipped 25 gas turbines in the quarter, a 32% increase from the prior year, with revenue gains across both heavy-duty and aeroderivative units.
Roughly 80% of the 100 gigawatts under contract is with traditional utility, independent power producer and industrial customers. The remaining 20% is explicitly tied to data center load.
Pricing on the company’s new gas turbine orders in the first half of 2026 is tracking 10 to 20 points higher on a dollar-per-kilowatt basis than fourth-quarter 2025 orders, Chief Executive Officer Scott Strazik told analysts.
Overall, gas turbine prices are projected to reach $600/kW by the end of 2027, nearly tripling from 2019 levels, according to a recent report from the consulting firm Wood Mackenzie.
The 2029 and 2030 slot crunch is real
Entering the first quarter, GE Vernova had roughly 10 gigawatts of available 2029 production capacity. After a quarter in which customers pulled aggressively into 2030 for scheduling reasons tied to engineering, procurement and construction timelines, the company now has approximately 10 GW remaining across 2029 and 2030 combined.
“We sold a lot of 2030 slots because the reality is we had a lot of customers looking at planning with EPC schedules and other dynamics needed the ’30 slot more than ’29,” Strazik said.
Directional lead times on new heavy-duty gas turbine orders are running at about three years, Strazik said. He cautioned that the turbine itself is often not the binding constraint on project delivery.
“At the end of the day, in many of the cases with these projects, the gas turbines are really not the gating item when you’re talking about a 3-year cycle from when a project starts, the EPC buildout, the permitting, the fuel availability,” he said.
Production capacity is moving in parallel. GE Vernova has installed more than 280 new machines across its gas power factories over the past 15 months and expects to reach 20 GW of annualized output by the third quarter of 2026, with further expansion to 24 GW targeted by 2028. The company will have added roughly 1,800 U.S. production workers across 2025 and 2026, with the largest share concentrated in gas power facilities.
